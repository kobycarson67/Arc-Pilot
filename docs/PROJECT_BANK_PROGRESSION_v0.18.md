# ARC Project Bank Progression & Timing — v0.18 Design Contract

## Purpose
Use real checkpoint activity to help the instructor understand how long projects actually take students, recommend appropriate next projects, and improve future pacing without turning ARC into an automatic gatekeeper.

## Difficulty
Project Bank templates may carry an instructor-defined difficulty level from 1–5.

- Level 1 — introductory fabrication with basic measurement, preparation, and joining.
- Level 2 — familiar processes with modest layout/fit-up demands.
- Level 3 — multi-step fabrication requiring consistent measurement, fit-up, welding, and independence.
- Level 4 — complex fabrication, tighter tolerances, process choices, or multiple skills/processes.
- Level 5 — advanced, highly independent work requiring planning, troubleshooting, precision, and/or multiple advanced processes.

Difficulty is descriptive, not a grade. The instructor may edit it at any time.

## Prerequisites and Recommended Next Projects
A template may reference prerequisite competencies, prerequisite projects, and/or a minimum recommended difficulty progression.

ARC may label projects as Recommended, Developing Toward, or Instructor Override Available. It must not permanently hard-lock a project. The instructor always retains assignment authority.

A recommendation must be explainable, for example: "Recommended because WT-M1 Measurement and WT-M3 Squareness & Accuracy are Proficient and Level 2 prerequisite projects are complete."

## Time Measurement
ARC measures project duration in eligible shop opportunities, not simple calendar days.

An eligible project opportunity must be explicitly recorded and must pass attendance safeguards. Excused, School Activity, and No Class do not count as eligible opportunities.

Calendar age alone must never be presented as student working time.

## Completion Sample
A historical timing sample is valid only when a project has:

1. a Project Bank/template identity,
2. checkpoint tracking initialized,
3. a reliable first eligible opportunity,
4. a reliable completion/verification point, and
5. enough activity records to calculate eligible opportunities.

Legacy projects without checkpoint activity are not retroactively guessed into timing samples.

## Observed Estimates
Project Bank templates may show both:

- Instructor Estimate — manually entered expected eligible shop periods/range.
- ARC Observed Range — calculated from valid completed historical samples.

ARC must show the sample size with any observed estimate. Until enough samples exist, display "Not enough data yet" rather than a misleading range.

Initial reliability rule: do not publish a student-facing or planning-range estimate with fewer than 5 valid completions. Preserve the underlying samples so this threshold can be revisited after pilot data exists.

Prefer robust ranges/medians over a simple mean so one unusually fast or slow completion does not distort planning.

## Skill-Level Timing
Future estimates may segment historical completion time by demonstrated skill level when enough data exists in each group. Example groups may align to ARC competency evidence such as Developing, Proficient, and Advanced.

Do not infer a student's global "ability." Any segmentation must be based on relevant documented competency evidence at the time of the project and must remain explainable.

If a subgroup lacks enough valid samples, ARC should fall back to the broader project estimate or say "Not enough data yet."

## Checkpoint Timing
ARC may calculate eligible opportunities and elapsed working windows between universal checkpoints:

1. Plan & Understand
2. Material Preparation
3. Layout & Measurement
4. Fit-Up
5. Tack & Pre-Weld Check
6. Welding / Joining
7. Finish & Cleanup
8. Final Inspection

This can reveal where students commonly spend time or encounter difficulty without converting checkpoint duration into a grade.

## Dynamic Pacing and Materials — Future
Once enough reliable historical data exists, ARC may use observed project timing to support:

- forecasted project completion windows,
- Open Shop next-project recommendations,
- likely upcoming material demand,
- project sequencing,
- instructor planning and Dynamic Pacing.

These are planning aids, not automatic academic decisions.

## Data Integrity Rules
- No estimate from assignment age alone.
- No false opportunity from Excused, School Activity, or No Class.
- No retroactive invented checkpoint history.
- No automatic grade from project speed.
- Faster completion is not automatically better performance.
- Difficulty does not alter rubric scores by itself.
- Instructor override remains available for project assignment/recommendation.
- Historical estimates must retain sample count and provenance.

## v0.18 Scope
Build and preserve the data foundation now: checkpoint identity, checkpoint activity, eligible opportunities, progress/review/verification dates, and Project Bank template identity.

Do not yet build automatic unlocks, skill-level estimates, material forecasting, or student-facing time targets. Those features should be activated only after sufficient real classroom data exists to validate the model.
