# Oelrichs School Instructional Walk-through Rubric Faithful Transcription

Source photographs: `../source_material/452.jpg`, `453.jpg`, `454.jpg`, and `455.jpg`.

Administrative fields: Month of; Administrator; Date Reviewed; Lesson Reflections completed and submitted on; Notes/Comments.

Rating columns: Advanced (4), Proficient (3), Basic (2), Below Basic (1).

## Domain 3 #1 Learning Targets

- **Advanced (4):** Learning target(s) are posted and clearly communicated in student-friendly language throughout the lesson. Students can explain what they are learning. Instructional activities are intentionally aligned to the target.
- **Proficient (3):** Learning target posted in student friendly language and is referred to only at the beginning of the lesson. Students do not demonstrate understanding of the target as it is not aligned to student tasks.
- **Basic (2):** Learning target is present; but not written in student-friendly language or referred to throughout the lesson. It appears to only be partially aligned to student tasks.
- **Below Basic (1):** Learning target is not evident or referred to within the lesson and it does not seem to be directly aligned to an identifiable learning outcome.

## Domain 3 #2 Success Criteria Student Clarity

- **Advanced (4):** Success criteria are posted, clearly communicated and actively used throughout the lesson. Students know exactly what successful learning looks like and use the criteria to monitor their own progress.
- **Proficient (3):** Success criteria are posted and often verbally referred to; students demonstrate a general understanding of what they are expected to do.
- **Basic (2):** Success Criteria are posted and referred to but unclear or are not actively used to guide student learning. Students have very limited understanding of what success looks like.
- **Below Basic (1):** Success criteria are not posted; or verbally reviewed. Students are unable to articulate what they are expected to accomplish.

## Domain 3 #3 Teacher Actions Instructional Practices

- **Advanced (4):** Teacher provides clear, explicit instruction; models thinking; uses purposeful questioning; scaffolds learning; monitors individual students; provides actionable feedback; and adjusts instruction in response to student understanding. Teacher is active; instructing; monitoring and re-teaching.
- **Proficient (3):** Teacher provides clear instruction, models and questions and regularly monitors student understanding; providing some feedback. Teacher does not remain active during the entire lesson.
- **Basic (2):** Instruction is primarily teacher-directed with limited modeling, questioning, monitoring or feedback. Adjustments to student needs are inconsistent.
- **Below Basic (1):** Instruction lacks clarity or purpose. Teacher provides limited monitoring, feedback, scaffolding, or response to student needs. Teacher is passive for a part of the lesson.

## Domain 3 #4 Student Engagement and Ownership

- **Advanced (4):** All or nearly all students are cognitively engaged through purposeful discussion, questioning, collaboration, movement, personal choice, problem solving, higher-level thinking and/or project-based learning.
- **Proficient (3):** Most students actively participate in meaningful learning activities aligned to the learning target. Students have opportunities to respond, collaborate, and demonstrate their thinking.
- **Basic (2):** Student engagement is inconsistent. Some students actively participate while others are passive. Activities may emphasize task completion rather than thinking and learning.
- **Below Basic (1):** Instruction is primarily teacher driven. Most students are passive, disengaged, or completing activities with little evidence of meaningful cognitive engagement.

## Domain 3 #5 Formative and Summative Assessment Strategies

- **Advanced (4):** Teacher frequently checks understanding from all students throughout the lesson, analyzes student responses, provides immediate feedback and adjusts instruction, pacing or support based on evidence of learning.
- **Proficient (3):** Teacher uses multiple checks for understanding during instruction and uses student responses to monitor progress toward the learning target.
- **Basic (2):** Checks for understanding are limited, relying only on a few students, or occur primarily at the end of instruction. Evidence has limited impact on instruction.
- **Below Basic (1):** Instruction is essentially the same for all students regardless of readiness, learning needs, or demonstrated understanding.

## Domain 2 #1 Classroom Culture and the Learning Environment

- **Advanced (4):** Classroom reflects a culture of belonging, mutual respect and high expectations. Students feel safe asking questions, making mistakes, sharing ideas and collaborating. Most interactions are consistently positive and respectful.
- **Proficient (3):** Classroom is respectful, safe, organized and focused on learning. Positive teacher-student and student-student interactions are evident.
- **Basic (2):** Classroom expectations and relationships are generally appropriate but inconsistent. Some students may be reluctant to participate or take academic risks.
- **Below Basic (1):** Classroom environment interferes with learning. Expectations are unclear and/or interactions demonstrate limited respect, belonging, safety, or academic focus.

## Domain 2 #2 Classroom Management

- **Advanced (4):** Routines, procedures, transitions, materials and student behavior maximize instructional time. Students demonstrate independence and understand expectations with minimal teacher direction.
- **Proficient (3):** Classroom routines and expectations are established and generally support efficient use of instructional time. Students may need constant reminders.
- **Basic (2):** Routines and expectations are inconsistently implemented. Transitions, behavior or organizational issues periodically interrupt learning.
- **Below Basic (1):** Lack of established routines expectations or behavior supports results in significant loss of instructional time.

## Evidence observed checklist

- Learning target posted and verbally identified
- Success Criteria aligned and explained
- Students can articulate their learning
- Active student responses
- Student-to-student discourse
- Higher-order questioning/thinking
- Teacher continually checks for understanding
- Teacher provides clear, purposeful and effective feedback
- Instruction is adjusted based on student response
- Teacher differentiates and scaffolds learning to meet diverse student need
- Overall positive classroom culture
- Effective routines and transitions
- Students respond positively to teacher re-direction
- Observer Notes
